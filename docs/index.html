EFU: Enforcing Federated Unlearning via Functional Encryption

Enforce unlearning while keeping the request private

Samaneh Mohammadi · Vasileios Tsouvalas · Iraklis Symeonidis · Ali Balador · Tanir Ozcelebi · Francesco Flammini · Nirvana Meratnia

PaperPublished versionCodeRepositoryPosterCite
OverviewMethodResultsCitation
The challenge

A client can compute an unlearning update, yet the server may identify or omit it during aggregation.

The contribution

EFU binds compressed learning and unlearning updates to authorized encrypted aggregation, with auxiliary losses intended to reduce observable unlearning traces.

Method overview · Based on the paper

The idea

At a glance

A server should not silently drop an unlearning update from the agreed federated aggregation. EFU binds encrypted updates to authorized aggregation and lets clients submit learning or unlearning updates through the same protocol. Its experiments measure the trade-off between forgetting selected information and retaining model utility.

  • Treat secure aggregation and the unlearning objective as separate components: the former constrains computation, while the latter determines behavioral forgetting.
  • Retention and forgetting must be assessed together, using retraining as a reference rather than treating low accuracy as an erasure certificate.
  • The empirical privacy evidence concerns specific parameter, timing, and communication signals on CIFAR-10.

Read the abstract

Federated unlearning (FU) algorithms allow clients in federated settings to exercise their “right to be forgotten” by removing the influence of their data from a collaboratively trained model. Existing FU methods maintain data privacy by performing unlearning locally on the client-side and sending targeted updates to the server without exposing forgotten data; yet they often rely on server-side cooperation, revealing the client's intent and identity without enforcement guarantees - compromising autonomy and unlearning privacy. In this work, we propose EFU (Enforced Federated Unlearning), a cryptographically enforced FU framework that enables clients to initiate unlearning while concealing its occurrence from the server. Specifically, EFU leverages functional encryption to bind encrypted updates to specific aggregation functions, ensuring the server can neither perform unauthorized computations nor detect or skip unlearning requests. To further mask behavioral and parameter shifts in the aggregated model, we incorporate auxiliary unlearning losses based on adversarial examples and parameter importance regularization. Extensive experiments show that EFU achieves near-random accuracy on forgotten data while maintaining performance comparable to full retraining across datasets and neural architectures - all while concealing unlearning intent from the server. Furthermore, we demonstrate that EFU is agnostic to the underlying unlearning algorithm, enabling secure, function-hiding, and verifiable unlearning for any client-side FU mechanism that issues targeted updates.

The approach

How it works

  1. 01

    Choose what to forget

    Clients train on retained data or compute targeted unlearning updates locally. The proposed objective combines gradient ascent on forgotten data with adversarial examples and parameter-importance regularization.

  2. 02

    Bind updates to aggregation

    Weight clustering compresses each update before functional encryption. Clients submit encrypted centroids, their weight mappings, and partial decryption keys tied to the round and aggregation function.

  3. 03

    Reveal only the aggregate

    The server expands the encrypted centroids and combines the clients’ functional keys to recover the agreed aggregate. Learning and unlearning follow the same encrypted update protocol.

Experiments & evidence

What the results show

Read retained accuracy alongside forgotten-class or forgotten-task accuracy; neither metric establishes exact data erasure or universal undetectability.

Data and models
CIFAR-10, 30-identity LFW, and 62-class FEMNIST; pretrained ResNet-18 and ViT-B/32.
Federated setting
Flower simulations with Dirichlet concentration 0.1, 64 weight clusters, and a 256-bit DMCFE key; 1 local learning epoch and 5 unlearning epochs.
Compared with
Full retraining, SGA-EWC, PGD, FedOSD, and their EFU integrations; sample-, class-, and task-level experiments.
Privacy evidence
DMCFE-based proof sketches plus CIFAR-10 plots of aggregate parameter drift, local execution time, and message size; no tabulated detector accuracy.

Scope & limitations

  • The stated threat model trusts clients to generate keys and perform unlearning correctly. The decryption argument assumes the agreed function and no unauthorized alternative key; it does not force a server to continue training or deploy the returned model.
  • Encryption hides ciphertext contents under DMCFE assumptions. The server also sees mappings and aggregate outputs; the paper conditions update-type indistinguishability on indistinguishable function outputs, and its side-channel plots are not a general detection test.
  • The sample-wise rows report retained test accuracy but no forgotten-sample metric. Appendix C defines a prediction-gap proxy, but no numerical prediction-gap or membership-inference audit is reported.
  • The runtime comparison generates adversarial examples offline. Its measured local update time is not a complete accounting of unlearning preparation cost.
